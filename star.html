The interview

Four questions, roughly five minutes each, in front of a panel of nephrology Heads of Department from the Queensland training sites. Three are behavioural and scored against Patient Focus, Communication and Staff Supervision; the fourth is the clinical vignette (Clinical Expertise). They are marking a safe, reflective first-year advanced trainee, not a subspecialist.

STAR, with a fifth step
S
Situation
Set the scene in one or two sentences. Who, where, when. Resist over-explaining the backstory.
T
Task
Your specific responsibility or the challenge you faced. Make it clear what was on you.
A
Action
What you did, step by step. This is the bulk of the answer. Say "I", not "we", and show your reasoning and the behaviour they are scoring.
R
Result
What happened. Make it concrete, and give credit to the team where it is due.
Ref
Reflection
What you learned or would do differently. Panels weight this heavily because it shows insight. Do not skip it.
What good looks like

Safe and patient-centred, a team player who escalates appropriately and knows the limits of their role, and someone who reflects honestly. They want to hear that you sought help at the right moment, not that you handled everything alone.

Common pitfalls

Rambling setup, no result. Keep Situation short and always land the Result and Reflection.

Hiding in "we". If your own action is invisible, they cannot score you. Say what you did.

Wrong story for the competency. A great story that does not demonstrate the behaviour asked scores poorly. Match the story to the question.

Being vague. Use one specific incident, not "generally I would...".

No reflection. The reflective close is where marks are won.

Prep strategy

Build six to eight concrete stories from your career (RBWH, Mayo, PhD, teaching, QI). Tag each to the competencies it can serve; most flex across two or three. Rehearse out loud to time: aim for a tight 90 seconds to two minutes on the core answer, then handle the probes. Prepare the Result and Reflection for every story in advance.
